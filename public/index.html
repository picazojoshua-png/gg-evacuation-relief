Our Mission

To give residents of Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park, and Westminster affected by the May 2026 GKN Aerospace chemical incident a place to document what the evacuation cost them and to share their experiences in their own words — creating a public record of the community's impact and the questions residents want answered.

What Happened

On May 21, 2026, a 34,000-gallon storage tank at the GKN Aerospace facility (12122 Western Ave, Garden Grove) — one of three tanks at the site — overheated and began venting methyl methacrylate (MMA), a volatile, flammable industrial chemical used to make acrylic plastics. According to reporting on the incident, the cooling system for the tank containing methyl methacrylate was compromised, and the tank's valve seized due to the chemical reaction inside the container.

Authorities ordered the evacuation of surrounding cities — a number that grew over the course of the crisis to as many as 50,000 people forced from their homes. The Orange County District Attorney has launched an investigation into the incident, stating officials will determine whether the company "has been derelict in its duties to protect the public." A class-action lawsuit has already been filed on behalf of evacuated residents.

What MMA Exposure Can Do

According to the U.S. EPA and OSHA, breathing methyl methacrylate vapors can affect human health. Reported respiratory symptoms in humans include chest tightness, difficulty breathing, coughing, wheezing, and reduced lung function. Higher exposures can cause dizziness, lightheadedness, and loss of consciousness, and exposure can irritate the eyes, skin, nose, and throat. Neurological symptoms such as headache, lethargy, and a sensation of heaviness in the arms and legs have also been reported following short-term exposure. Methyl methacrylate is classified as a flammable, reactive chemical and a fire and explosion hazard. This is the chemical that tens of thousands of residents were told to flee.

Why This Platform Exists

Families across these cities incurred real costs — hotels, food, pet boarding, medical needs, transportation — and many still don't know when they can return or who, if anyone, will help with those costs. This platform exists to document that impact and to raise the questions residents are asking:

  • If safety or redundancy systems exist at this facility, why didn't they prevent this — and if they failed, why did they fail?
  • When was the storage tank last inspected, and were any issues identified?
  • Why is a facility storing large quantities of a hazardous, flammable chemical located near homes and schools?
  • If the City had an emergency response plan, why has the situation escalated the way it has, and why were so many families left to arrange shelter, costs, and care on their own?
  • Who is responsible for the costs that displaced families are now absorbing?
  • What is being done to ensure this does not happen again?

We don't claim to have the answers. We believe these questions deserve answers — from GKN, from the City, and from the agencies responsible for oversight.

What This Platform Does

  • Documents resident experiences in their own words
  • Records the financial impact through self-reported evacuation costs
  • Raises questions that residents believe deserve public attention and official response
  • Creates a shared record that residents may choose to use in seeking assistance, including disaster-relief applications and conversations with elected officials and the press
